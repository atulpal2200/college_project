In **Section VIII: “Evaluation Metrics”** (pp. 13–14, Table 22) of _MCQs Generation With Large Language Models: A Survey of Methodologies, Evolution, and Open Research Issues_ (Anwar et al., 2026), the authors analyze how automatically generated Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs) are evaluated across current literature [Section VIII, pp. 13–14].

---

### **1. Evaluation Framework**

The authors establish that evaluating AI-generated MCQs requires a multi-layered framework combining **automatic metrics**, **human evaluation**, and **psychometric analysis** [Section VIII, p. 13].

The paper outlines four recurring quality dimensions assessed across these layers [Section VIII, p. 13]:

- **Relevance:** Alignment of the question and stem with the target concept or source text.
- **Difficulty:** How challenging the item is for target learners.
- **Distractor Plausibility:** How convincing and functional the incorrect options are.
- **Fluency & Naturalness:** Whether the phrasing is grammatically correct and error-free.

---

### **2. Automatic Evaluation**

Automatic evaluation utilizes algorithms to compare generated questions or options against reference text or source material [Section VIII-A, p. 13].

As summarized in **Table 22** (p. 14) and **Section VIII-A** (pp. 13–14), the paper discusses the following automated metrics:

- **N-Gram Overlap Metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-N, ROUGE-L, ROUGE-QAG):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Lexical and syntactic surface similarity (word/phrase overlap) between generated questions and human reference questions [Section VIII-A, p. 13; Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Advantages:_ Cheap, automated, highly scalable, and standard for benchmark comparisons [Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Limitations:_ Evaluates surface text matching rather than educational value. Scores show **poor correlation with semantic correctness**; a generated question can score high on BLEU/ROUGE while being factually wrong, ambiguous, or pedagogically useless [Section VIII-A, p. 13; Section X-F, p. 15; Table 22, p. 14].
- **Semantic Similarity Metrics (Cosine Similarity, BERTScore, Word Mover's Distance [WMD]):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Conceptual closeness and semantic alignment of stems and distractors beyond exact word matching [Section VIII-A, p. 13; Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Advantages:_ Captures meaning and contextual synonymy beyond surface n-grams [Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Limitations:_ Heavily dependent on the representation quality of the underlying embedding model [Table 22, p. 14].
- **Precision-Based Metrics (Precision at k / P@k):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Evaluates distractor quality by assessing the proportion of top-\(k\) generated distractors that meet similarity or plausibility thresholds [Section VIII-A, p. 13; Table 22, p. 14].
- **Retrieval-Based Accuracy Differential (RAD):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Evaluates the discriminative power of generated items using an information retrieval framework [Section VIII-A, p. 13].
- **Extractive Span Evaluation (Exact Match [EM], F1-Score, Partial Match [PM]):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Objective correctness of extracted answer keys against ground-truth text spans [Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Limitations:_ EM is overly strict and penalizes minor, valid phrasing variations [Table 22, p. 14].
- **Paraphrasing Metrics (METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Assesses question stem novelty and syntactic divergence from raw source text [Table 22, p. 14].
- **Diversity Metrics (Entropy \(H(Q)\), Self-BLEU):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Quantifies the structural variety and non-redundancy across a generated question set [Table 22, p. 14].

### **3. Human Evaluation**

Human evaluation remains the primary gold standard for evaluating qualities that automated algorithms cannot measure [Section VIII-B, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14]:

- **Expert/Human Review:** Subject-matter experts evaluate items using Likert scales (e.g., 1–5), rubrics, or binary _Accept/Reject_ choices [Section VIII-B, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Assessed Criteria:_ Overall item quality, factual correctness, instructional relevance, clarity, distractor plausibility, and grammatical fluency [Section VIII-B, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Strengths:_ Captures subtle domain nuances, validates factual accuracy, and verifies alignment with learning objectives (LOs) [Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Limitations:_ Subjective, slow, expensive, and unscalable [Table 22, p. 14].
- **Inter-Rater Agreement (Gwet's AC1, Fleiss' Kappa, Cohen's Kappa):**
    - _What it Measures:_ Quantifies the statistical consistency and reliability of scores assigned across multiple human raters [Table 22, p. 14].
    - _Limitations:_ Inter-rater agreement varies significantly across studies, and metrics like Fleiss' Kappa can be weak when rating distributions are dominated by a single label [Section VIII-B, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].
- **Indistinguishability / Turing Test:**
    - _What it Measures:_ Assesses perceived human-likeness by testing whether reviewers can differentiate AI-generated MCQs from human-authored exam items [Table 22, p. 14].

---

### **4. Psychometric Evaluation**

Psychometric analysis provides empirical proof of an item's actual assessment value when administered to learners [Section VIII-C, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14]:

- **Theoretical Frameworks:** Classical Test Theory (CTT) and Item Response Theory (IRT) (e.g., Rasch model) [Section VIII-C, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].
- **Core Metrics:**
    - **Item Difficulty (ID):** Measures the proportion of students who answer an item correctly (p-value or Rasch difficulty score) [Section VIII-C, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].
    - **Discriminative Power / Item Discrimination (DP):** Measured via point-biserial correlation; evaluates how effectively an MCQ differentiates high-performing students from low-performing students [Section VIII-C, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].
    - **Distractor Statistics / Distractor Effectiveness:** Evaluates whether distractors successfully attract students with partial understanding rather than being ignored by 100% of test-takers [Section VIII-C, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].
- **Crucial Prerequisite Boundary:** Psychometric evaluation **requires actual student response data from a sizable learner cohort**. It **cannot** be conducted during early system design or real-time pre-filtering [Section VIII-C, p. 14; Table 22, p. 14].

### **5. Comparative Summary of Evaluation Approaches**

The table below synthesizes the paper's comparison across the three evaluation paradigms [Section VIII, pp. 13–14; Table 22, p. 14]:

|Paradigm|Primary Purpose|Key Metrics / Techniques|Major Strengths|Major Limitations|Data Requirements|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Automatic Evaluation**|Fast, large-scale surface text & retrieval checks.|BLEU, ROUGE-L, Cosine Sim, BERTScore, P@k, Exact Match, RAD.|Cheap, automated, highly scalable, reproducible.|Poor correlation with factual correctness and learning value; penalizes valid paraphrases.|Generated MCQ + Source Text / Gold Reference Questions.|
|**Human Evaluation**|Qualitative verification of correctness, fluency & educational value.|Rubrics, Likert scales, Binary Accept/Reject, Cohen's/Fleiss' Kappa.|Gold standard; captures domain nuance; verifies factual correctness & distractor plausibility.|Subjective, slow, expensive, unscalable; inter-rater variation.|Generated MCQ + Source PDF + Human Domain Expert.|
|**Psychometric Evaluation**|Quantitative proof of item behavior in real exams.|Item Difficulty (Rasch), Discrimination (Point-Biserial), Distractor Stats.|Empirical proof of test validity and student discrimination.|Cannot be run in real time or during early development; unscalable prior to testing.|**Student Response Data** from a sizable test-taking cohort.|

### **6. Limitations and Research Gaps Identified in the Paper**

1. **No Standardized Evaluation Benchmark:** Section X-C (_"Standardized Evaluation and Benchmarking"_, p. 14) explicitly states that a **consistent, universally accepted evaluation framework and benchmark dataset for automatically generated MCQs is completely missing**.
2. **Metric-Pedagogy Disconnect:** Automatic metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) evaluate surface text similarity, not teaching effectiveness. A model can achieve state-of-the-art BLEU scores while generating factually incorrect or trivial items [Section X-F, p. 15].
3. **Inter-Rater Inconsistency:** Human evaluation rubrics lack standardization, resulting in inconsistent inter-rater agreement across studies [Section VIII-B, p. 14].
4. **Inaccessibility of Psychometric Data:** Psychometric validation is rarely performed during system development due to the difficulty of obtaining large-scale student response data [Section VIII-C, p. 14].

### **7. Application to Our Project (LLM + RAG Professor MVP)**

#### **A. Paper-Supported Recommendations**

- **Adopt a Hybrid Evaluation Strategy:** Combine automated pre-filtering for computational efficiency with human professor review for factual and pedagogical verification [Section VIII-C, p. 14].
- **Avoid Relying on BLEU/ROUGE for Quality Gates:** Do not use BLEU or ROUGE as acceptance metrics for production MCQs; rely on human expert judgment for correctness [Section X-F, p. 15].
- **Incorporate Human-in-the-Loop Verification:** Position the system as an assistant preparing draft items for professor approval (_Approve / Edit / Reject_) [Section X-E, p. 15].

#### **B. Assistant Recommendations (Explicitly Labeled as My Own Suggestion)**

- _Suggestion:_ Implement a **three-stage evaluation pipeline** tailored to your MVP:
    1. **Stage 1 (Automated Pre-Review Rules):** Automatically filter out broken JSON, duplicate option choices, or stems that lack text overlap with the retrieved RAG PDF chunk before rendering items to the professor.
    2. **Stage 2 (Professor Approval Dashboard):** Present filtered MCQs to the professor to review factual accuracy, distractor plausibility, and course alignment.
    3. **Stage 3 (Future Psychometric Logging):** When student quiz submissions are introduced in v2, log student response selections to compute empirical Item Difficulty (\(p\)-value) and flag non-functional distractors.

|Evaluation Layer|Criterion|Measurable Indicator / Metric|Operational Definition|

||**Grammatical Fluency**|Error-Free Phrasing Check|Confirms seamless stem-option grammatical agreement.|

||**Item Discrimination (DP)**|Point-Biserial Correlation|Measures correlation between item score and total test score.|
||**Distractor Functionality**|Selection Rate per Distractor|Flags non-functional distractors selected by 0% of students.|

### **Five Key Takeaways for MVP Implementation**

1. **BLEU/ROUGE Are Not Quality Gates:** Automated n-gram metrics measure text overlap, not factual accuracy or pedagogical value; do not use them to auto-approve MCQs [Section X-F, p. 15].
2. **Automate What Is Computable First:** Use automated pre-checks (JSON format, option duplication, RAG text-span overlap) to filter out broken draft items before showing them to professors [Section VII-C, p. 13; Table 22, p. 14].
3. **Professor Approval is the True Gold Standard:** Human professor review (_Approve / Edit / Reject_) is the essential quality gate for factual correctness and distractor plausibility [Section VIII-B, p. 14; Section X-E, p. 15].
4. **Psychometrics Require Student Data:** Item difficulty, discrimination, and distractor effectiveness cannot be evaluated prior to classroom testing; defer psychometric metrics to post-exam analytics [Section VIII-C, p. 14].
5. **Adopt a Multi-Layered Hybrid Strategy:** Combining automated pre-filtering, professor oversight, and future student response logging provides the most reliable evaluation pipeline [Section VIII-C, p. 14].
