### **1. Breakdown of MCQ Generation Methodologies**

#### **A. Foundational Approaches: Rule-Based & Template-Based**

- **How It Works:** Rule-based systems apply handcrafted linguistic patterns, Part-of-Speech (POS) tags, or syntactic parsers to source text. Template-based systems populate predefined sentence slots with entities extracted from text or databases.
- **Advantages:** High structural control, predictable outputs, and high execution speed (e.g., generating 1,600 medical MCQs in under two seconds).
- **Limitations:** Extremely rigid, brittle, domain-specific, and difficult to scale. Adapting to a new subject requires manually authoring new rule sets .
- **How It Differs:** Strictly deterministic; does not learn patterns from data or context.
- **Representative Examples:** Coniam (1997) (vocabulary cloze tests), Kıyak et al. (2023) (medical clinical reasoning), Flor & Riordan (2018) (semantic role labeling rules). _(Section V-A, p. 7; Table 6 & 7)_

#### **B. Natural Language Processing (NLP)-Based Approaches**

- **How It Works:** Uses shallow parsing, POS tagging, dependency trees, TF-IDF, and term extraction to select informative sentences and identify target answer keys.
- **Advantages:** Functions as the core computational toolkit for extracting text features; adaptable across languages (e.g., Basque, Chinese).
- **Limitations:** Requires complex multi-stage pipelines; highly dependent on parser accuracy; struggles to generate complex stems or deep conceptual questions.
- **How It Differs:** Processes the syntactic structure of raw text directly without requiring predefined templates or trained neural weights.
- **Representative Examples:** Aldabe et al. (2006) (ArikIturri for Basque), Nwafor & Onyenwe (2021) (TF-IDF and POS tags). _(Section V-B, pp. 7–8; Table 8)_

#### **C. Data-Driven Approaches: Traditional ML & Deep Learning (DL)**

- **How It Works:** Traditional ML uses statistical classifiers (Naïve Bayes, Support Vector Machines, CRFs) to select sentences and gap positions. DL uses neural sequence models (RNNs, LSTMs, Transformers like T5) to learn language patterns from annotated datasets.
- **Advantages:** ML automates feature selection without explicit rules. DL captures long-range context, improving sentence fluency and grammatical coherence.
- **Limitations:** ML requires heavy manual feature engineering and external dictionaries (WordNet). DL requires massive annotated Question-Answering datasets (SQuAD, RACE) and remains dependent on dataset quality.
- **How It Differs:** Learns statistical probability distributions directly from training data rather than relying on static rules or templates.
- **Representative Examples:** Correia et al. (2012) (SVM feature engineering), Rodriguez-Torrealba et al. (2022) (T5 fine-tuned on SQuAD/RACE). _(Section V-C, pp. 8–9; Table 9 & 10)_

#### **D. Knowledge-Based / Ontology-Based Approaches**

- **How It Works:** Leverages formal, structured domain knowledge (Web Ontology Language / OWL, RDF knowledge graphs) mapping class hierarchies (_is-a_ relations) and properties.
- **Advantages:** High conceptual accuracy, logical soundness, and semantically grounded distractors (e.g., querying sibling classes under the same parent node).
- **Limitations:** Building comprehensive ontologies is expensive and labor-intensive; struggles with natural phrasing without separate natural language generation modules.
- **How It Differs:** Operates on explicit graph structures of real-world domain knowledge rather than raw text corpora or statistical probabilities.
- **Representative Examples:** Al-Yahya (2011, 2014) ("OntoQue"), Leo et al. (2019) (EMMET-OWL medical MCQs). _(Section V-D, p. 9; Table 11)_

#### **E. LLM-Based Approaches & Sub-Categories**

- **How It Works:** Uses large generative models (GPT-4, Gemini, PaLM, LLaMA) leveraging pre-trained comprehension, reasoning, and instruction-following capabilities. _(Section V-E, pp. 9–11)_
- **Advantages:** Unmatched linguistic fluency, deep contextual awareness, high scalability across subjects, and minimal setup time.
- **Limitations:** Prone to factual hallucinations, superficial word-level distractors, cognitive level drift, and opaque "black box" generation.
- **Five Sub-Categories (Section V-E1–E5):**
    1. **Prompting LLMs:** Uses natural language instructions (zero-shot, few-shot, multi-stage, Chain-of-Thought) without changing model weights. _(Table 13)_
    2. **Fine-Tuning LLMs:** Retrains model parameters on specialized MCQ datasets (e.g., T5 fine-tuned on DG-RACE) to customize output format or domain style. _(Table 12)_
    3. **Pipeline & Modular Approaches:** Chains multiple models sequentially (e.g., RoBERTa for Bloom's taxonomy alignment -> GPT-3.5 for question generation) . _(Table 14)_
    4. **Multi-Agent Systems:** Multiple specialized LLM agents (generator, critic, evaluator) collaborate in roles simulating human item-writing workflows. _(Table 15)_
    5. **Hybrid LLM Approaches:** Combines LLMs with rules, classical NLP (POS, WordNet), or RAG. _(Table 16 & 17)_

### **2. Role of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in MCQ Generation**

- **How It Works:** RAG retrieves relevant context text passages from an external document repository or vector database and injects them into the LLM prompt at inference time.
- **Primary Role:** Directly addresses the primary weakness of LLMs—factual hallucinations and knowledge cutoffs—by strictly constraining question stems, keys, and distractors to verified source passages.
- **Representative Examples:** Hang et al. (2024) ("MCQGen") (GPT-4 + RAG + self-refinement for computer science), Kıyak et al. (2024) (Custom ChatGPT + RAG for past exams). _(Section V-E2 & V-E3, pp. 10–11)_

### **3. Key Methodological Distinctions: Prompting vs. Fine-Tuning vs. RAG**

|Aspect|Pretrained LLM via Prompting|Fine-Tuning an LLM|Combining LLM with RAG|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Model Weights**|**Frozen** (Unchanged).|**Updated** on domain dataset.|**Frozen** (Unchanged).|
|**Primary Advantage**|Immediate, zero-setup execution across tasks .|Customizes output syntax/format and improves task-specific distractor style.|Eliminates factual hallucinations by anchoring context to real documents.|
|**Primary Weakness**|Relies on internal parametric memory; prone to hallucinations and superficial distractors.|Expensive to train; static (doesn't fix knowledge cutoffs or document updates).|Dependent on retrieval chunk quality and vector search accuracy.|
|**Adaptability to New PDFs**|Generic knowledge only; cannot read unprovided internal PDFs.|Requires retraining or re-fine-tuning on new document sets.|**Instantaneous**; retrieves from any uploaded PDF dynamically at inference time.|

### **4. Connecting Findings to Your MVP**

Your MVP generates single-answer MCQs from professor-uploaded PDFs/text using an **LLM + RAG** architecture paired with automated quality checks and a professor approval interface:

1. **Why LLM + RAG is the Correct Architecture for the MVP:**
    - **Prevents Factual Hallucinations:** Raw LLMs hallucinate plausible-sounding errors. RAG forces the LLM to ground the stem and answer key directly in the uploaded lecture PDF.
    - **Zero-Setup Subject Adaptability:** Unlike ontologies or rule-based models that require months of per-subject setup, LLM + RAG handles any academic discipline (from medicine to computer science) instantly.
2. **Validating the Professor-in-the-Loop Workflow:**
    - The survey repeatedly highlights that LLMs generate superficial distractors and lack transparent reasoning. Your professor approval dashboard directly operationalizes the paper's recommendation for **Human–AI Collaboration**.
3. **Future Work (Fine-Tuning + RAG):**
    - Retraining open-source models (like LLaMA-3 or T5) on MCQ datasets + RAG is appropriately designated as future work. Fine-tuning helps customize local JSON formatting and distractor style, but RAG alone solves the immediate MVP requirement of document grounding.

---

### **Three Most Important Methodological Insights for Your Project**

1. **RAG is Non-Negotiable for Factual Reliability:** Raw LLM prompting produces hallucinations; RAG is essential to anchor question stems and keys directly to the professor's PDF content.
2. **Multi-Stage / Self-Refinement Prompting Fixes Distractor Quality:** Because LLMs naturally default to superficial word associations, prompts must use multi-step instructions (e.g., extract key -> identify student misconceptions -> draft distractors -> self-critique) to produce plausible options.
3. **Professor Approval is the True Gold Standard:** Automated metrics (like BLEU or ROUGE) measure surface text overlap rather than educational validity. A human professor review interface (_Approve / Edit / Reject_) provides necessary quality control before classroom deployment.
