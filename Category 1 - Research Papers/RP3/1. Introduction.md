Here is an analysis of Section 1 (**“Introduction,”** pp. 1–2) of the paper _**QuerIA: Adaptive Question Generation and Evaluation in Higher Education Using Large Language Models and Contextual Learning**_ (Badenes-Olmedo & Eyzaguirre, 2026).

---

### **1. Problem Statement**

- **Time and Cognitive Burden:** Designing high-quality educational questionnaires and providing timely feedback is a time-consuming and cognitively demanding task for educators, particularly in large-scale or diverse academic environments.
- **Pedagogical Importance:** Well-constructed questionnaires are essential not only to measure student understanding but also to foster active engagement, reinforce knowledge retention, and enable self-regulated learning.

---

### **2. Research Motivation**

- **Why LLMs are Promising:** Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) offer strong contextual understanding, generative capabilities, and few-shot learning strategies, making automated question generation (QG) increasingly feasible.
- **Limitations of Existing Systems:** Integration of LLM-based QG into real-world educational workflows remains limited due to four key factors:
    1. Reliance on **monolingual training**.
    2. Lack of **adaptability in question difficulty**.
    3. Minimal alignment with pedagogical frameworks such as **Bloom’s Taxonomy**.
    4. Limited deployment in real-world educational settings.

---

### **3. Proposed Solution: QuerIA**

- **System Definition:** QuerIA is an intelligent, web-based educational system that combines LLM-based generation with contextual document segmentation and an adaptive taxonomy to automatically generate and evaluate educational questions.
- **Main Capabilities & Objectives:**
    - Supports both **Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)** and **Open-Ended Questions (OEQs)**.
    - Offers **adjustable difficulty levels** aligned with cognitive dimensions.
    - Provides **real-time automated feedback** for student responses.
    - Tested in a real-world pilot study involving over 300 users across multiple faculties at _Universidad Politécnica de Madrid (UPM)_.

---

### **4. Research Questions (RQ)**

As explicitly stated in Section 1 (pp. 1–2):

- **RQ1:** _"How does the generation of questions using Bloom-aligned prompts vary in terms of difficulty distribution and cognitive demand?"_ _(Investigates how structured Bloom prompts affect question complexity and difficulty levels)._
- **Unnumbered Sub-Question 1:** _"1. What patterns of adoption and usage emerged across academic institutions and user profiles during the pilot deployment?"_ _(Investigates institutional adoption volume, user engagement, and faculty activity across the pilot study)._
- **RQ2:** _"How do users evaluate the clarity, alignment, and pedagogical value of system-generated questions?"_ _(Investigates user perceptions regarding the instructional quality, clarity, and relevance of generated items)._

_(Note: While Section 1 labels the questions as RQ1, item "1.", and RQ2, Section 5 later refers to these three focus areas sequentially as RQ1, RQ2, and RQ3)._

---

### **5. Research Contributions**

The authors claim **three main contributions** in Section 1 (p. 2):

1. **Methodological Integration:** Combining **semantic chunking** with **Bloom-aligned prompting** to achieve systematic, controllable levels of cognitive difficulty.
2. **Real-World Institutional Deployment:** Presenting a pilot evaluation involving **341 participants across 17 faculties** in a higher education setting.
3. **Open-Source Release:** Releasing both the **source code** and the **anonymized pilot dataset** as open resources for the community.

---

### **6. Novelty (QuerIA vs. Commercial LLMs with Ad Hoc Prompts)**

The paper explicitly distinguishes QuerIA from simple ad hoc commercial LLM prompting:

- **Institutional Design:** Explicitly architected as an institutional tool rather than an informal prompting script.
- **Embedded Pedagogical Logic:** Integrates **Bloom’s Taxonomy** into reusable, standardized prompt templates to systematically dictate cognitive difficulty.
- **Systematic Reproducibility:** Ensures consistent question generation standards across different academic domains.
- **Data Governance & Local Hardware:** Runs **locally on standard institutional hardware** without reliance on external cloud APIs, safeguarding student data privacy and enabling execution in resource-constrained environments.

### **7. Relevance to Our Project (Professor-Facing MVP)**

#### **A. Directly Relevant to Current Scope (MVP)**

- **Bloom-Aligned Prompting for Difficulty Control:** Incorporating explicit Bloom taxonomy directives into prompt templates to control question cognitive demand.
- **Contextual Document Chunking:** Preprocessing uploaded PDFs into coherent semantic units before generating questions.
- **Local / Private Execution & Data Sovereignty:** Running open-source models locally on institutional hardware to maintain complete data governance.

#### **B. Future Considerations (Post-MVP Scope)**

- **Open-Ended Questions (OEQs) & Automated Scoring:** Expanding beyond MCQs to generate free-text questions graded automatically with LLM rubrics.
- **Student Self-Assessment & Real-Time Feedback:** Opening the platform directly to students for real-time quiz practice and feedback.
- **Institutional Adoption Analytics:** Tracking usage patterns, faculty participation, and topic distributions across a university campus.

---

### **Five Key Takeaways for Literature Review**

1. **Pedagogical Alignment:** Embedding Bloom's taxonomy directly into prompt templates provides structured control over cognitive difficulty, overcoming the randomness of ad hoc prompting.
2. **Contextual Segmentation:** Segmenting source documents into semantic units ensures that questions are generated from coherent topical blocks rather than arbitrary text splits.
3. **Data Governance & Local LLMs:** Hosting open-source models locally on standard university hardware avoids commercial API costs and protects institutional data privacy.
4. **Dual Item Formats:** Combining MCQs and OEQs with automated LLM scoring expands formative assessment capabilities beyond simple recall testing.
5. **Empirical Real-World Validation:** Evaluating system deployment across 341 real users in 17 university faculties provides empirical proof of operational viability beyond synthetic datasets.
