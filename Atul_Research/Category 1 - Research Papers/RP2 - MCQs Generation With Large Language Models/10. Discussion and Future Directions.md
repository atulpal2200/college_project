In Section X, **“Discussion and Future Directions”** (printed pages 15–17) [Section X, pp. 15–17], Anwar et al. (2026) synthesize the critical unresolved challenges in automated MCQ generation, evaluate the technical limitations of Large Language Models (LLMs), and outline an actionable research roadmap for the field.

---

### **1. Major Research Gaps**

The authors explicitly highlight five core research gaps that hinder the real-world adoption of automated MCQ generation [Section X-F, pp. 16–17]:

1. **Factual Reliability & Hallucination Risk:** Unlike classical rule-based or ontology-driven systems that generate items strictly from verified knowledge structures, LLMs are prone to generating fluent but factually erroneous statements [Section X-A, p. 15; Section X-F, p. 16].
    - _Why It Matters:_ Factual errors destroy assessment validity and are dangerous in high-stakes technical fields (e.g., medicine, law, engineering) where precision is non-negotiable [Section X-A, p. 15; Section X-F, p. 16].
2. **Superficial Distractor Construction:** LLMs tend to select distractors based on surface-level word-association rather than modeling genuine learner misconceptions or logical fallacies [Section X-B, p. 15; Section X-F, p. 16].
    - _Why It Matters:_ Distractors that lack pedagogical grounding are easily eliminated by test-takers, reducing the MCQ to a trivial guessing task that fails to discriminate student mastery [Section X-B, p. 15; Section X-F, p. 16].
3. **Cognitive Level Drift & Difficulty Unpredictability:** Even when explicitly prompted for higher-order thinking (such as analysis or evaluation on Bloom's taxonomy), LLMs frequently drift back to lower-level factual recall (memorization) [Section X-D, p. 16; Section X-F, p. 16].
    - _Why It Matters:_ Unpredictable difficulty prevents instructors from assembling balanced exam forms or targeting specific learning objectives (LOs) [Section X-D, p. 16; Section X-F, p. 16].
4. **Metric-Pedagogy Disconnect & Lack of Benchmarks:** Standard automated metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE) evaluate surface n-gram text overlap rather than instructional value or factual truth [Section X-C, p. 16; Section X-F, p. 16].
    - _Why It Matters:_ A model can achieve high automated BLEU/ROUGE scores while generating pedagogically useless or factually wrong questions [Section X-C, p. 16; Section X-F, p. 16].
5. **Opaque "Black Box" Generation:** Classical rule-based and ontology systems offer transparent, traceable generation logic, whereas LLMs generate outputs opaquely [Section X-F, p. 16].
    - _Why It Matters:_ Opaque reasoning makes it difficult for educators to understand why an error occurred or systematically optimize model output [Section X-F, p. 16].

---

### **2. Limitations of Existing LLM-Based Methods**

Section X summarizes the technical boundaries of current LLM approaches across five operational areas [Section X-A–F, pp. 15–16]:

- **Factual Correctness:** High susceptibility to plausible-sounding hallucinations [Section X-A, p. 15; Section X-F, p. 16].
- **Distractor Quality:** Reliance on superficial word co-occurrence rather than real student misconception patterns [Section X-B, p. 15; Section X-F, p. 16].
- **Evaluation Reliability:** Over-reliance on surface text-matching metrics or subjective, unscalable human review [Section X-C, p. 16; Section X-F, p. 16].
- **Domain Generalization:** Models fine-tuned or prompted for one subject struggle to generalize across diverse academic fields without specialized data [Section X-F, p. 16; Section X-G, p. 17].
- **Human Supervision Necessity:** Due to unpredictable failure modes, fully automated deployment remains unsafe; continuous human expert oversight is mandatory [Section X-D, p. 16; Section X-E, p. 16].

---

### **3. Future Research Directions Proposed by the Authors**

The authors outline five strategic research directions to advance the field [Section X-A–G, pp. 15–17]:

1. **Integrating LLMs with External Knowledge Bases & Fact-Checkers:** Combining generative models with external knowledge bases (e.g., RAG or lightweight verification modules) to guarantee 100% factual grounding [Section X-A, p. 15].
2. **Human-in-the-Loop Teacher Collaboration:** Developing interactive platforms where educators refine AI drafts and provide real student error data to improve distractor modeling [Section X-B, p. 15; Section X-E, p. 16].
3. **Standardized Benchmarks & Reporting Standards:** Creating shared benchmark datasets and transparent protocols that evaluate cognitive depth, difficulty, and educational relevance [Section X-C, p. 16].
4. **Ethical Auditing & Bias Mitigation:** Establishing transparent auditing mechanisms to prevent social/cultural biases in generated scenarios and enforcing data protection standards (FERPA/GDPR) [Section X-D, p. 16].
5. **Cross-Domain Generalization & Cohesive Test Assembly:** Designing models that adapt across STEM/humanities and moving from isolated question generation toward assembling cohesive, multi-step exam forms [Section X-G, p. 17].

---

### **4. Evaluation and Benchmarking Concerns**

Section X-C (_"Standardized Evaluation and Benchmarking"_, p. 16) highlights major flaws in current evaluation research:

- **Dataset Disconnect:** Many benchmark datasets (e.g., SQuAD) were designed for Machine Reading Comprehension, not educational item writing [Section X-C, p. 16].
- **Metric Disconnect:** Automated translation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) measure word similarity rather than teaching effectiveness [Section X-C, p. 16; Section X-F, p. 16].
- **Lack of Reproducibility:** Unstandardized human review rubrics cause high inter-rater variance, preventing direct performance comparisons across published studies [Section X-C, p. 16].

### **6. Practical Implications for Source-Grounded MCQs**

To build a reliable, source-grounded MCQ system, the paper's findings dictate that developers must prioritize **Factual Reliability (Document Grounding)** and **Human Expert Verification** above all else [Section X-A, p. 15; Section X-E, p. 16]. Relying on unanchored LLM prompts or automated text-overlap metrics will lead to hallucinated answer keys and non-functional distractors [Section X-F, p. 16].

### **Five Key Takeaways for Project Implementation**

1. **RAG Eliminates the #1 Reported LLM Risk:** Using RAG to anchor stems and keys directly to uploaded PDF text directly solves the paper's primary concern regarding factual hallucinations [Section X-A, p. 15].
2. **Professor Review is Essential:** Position your system as a human-in-the-loop assistant; human professor approval (_Approve / Edit / Reject_) fills the quality assurance gap that automated metrics cannot measure [Section X-E, p. 16].
3. **Ignore BLEU/ROUGE for Quality Gates:** Automated n-gram overlap metrics measure surface word similarity, not educational value or factual truth [Section X-C, p. 16; Section X-F, p. 16].
4. **Design Prompts to Combat Distractor Superficiality:** Implement multi-step prompts (extract key → identify misconception → draft distractors) to prevent the LLM from defaulting to superficial word associations [Section X-B, p. 15; Section X-F, p. 16].
5. **Turn Professor Interactions into Your Future Dataset:** Logging professor edits and approvals on generated items creates a gold-standard, domain-aligned dataset for future model fine-tuning [Section X-C, p. 16].
