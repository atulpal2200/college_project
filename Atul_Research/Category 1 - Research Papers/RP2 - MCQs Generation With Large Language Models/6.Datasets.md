### **1. Dataset Purpose**

According to Section VI (_"Datasets"_, pp. 11–12), publicly available datasets serve three primary technical functions in automatic MCQ generation: **training**, **fine-tuning**, and **evaluation**.

The paper notes that datasets vary significantly in structure, directly dictating how models are constructed:

- **QA & Reading Comprehension Datasets (Indirect Setup):** Corpora like **SQuAD**, **Natural Questions (NQs)**, and **TriviaQA** originate from Question-Answering tasks. They require models to first extract a question-answer pair from text before applying an extra transformation pipeline to generate distractors and form an MCQ (Section VI, p. 11).
- **Native MCQ & Cloze Datasets (Direct Setup):** Datasets like **CLOTH** and **RACE** contain native cloze and multiple-choice items crafted by educators, providing a direct training setup for model fine-tuning and distractor generation (Section VI, p. 11; Section V-E1, p. 9).
- **Model Scale & Reasoning Drivers:** Large-scale datasets like **MS MARCO** (1M items) and **LearningQ** (230k items) enable training deep learning architectures, while multi-hop reasoning datasets like **HotpotQA** and **OpenBookQA** drive advanced model inference (Section VI, pp. 11–12).

### **3. Training vs. Evaluation Use**

- **Explicit Paper Scope:** The paper lists the 40 datasets in Table 20 as general resources for _"training, fine-tuning, and evaluation"_ (Section VI, p. 11). It **does not explicitly restrict each dataset into a rigid, mutually exclusive "training-only" or "evaluation-only" category**.
- **Usage Across Literature:** In the specific studies surveyed:
    - **Training/Fine-Tuning Datasets:** Datasets providing native MCQs or text-to-MCQ pairs (e.g., **RACE**, **DG-RACE**, **CLOTH**, **SQuAD**) are frequently used to fine-tune transformer models like T5, BART, or BERT encoders for question generation and distractor ranking (Section V-E1, p. 9; Table 12 & Table 16).
    - **Evaluation/Benchmarking Datasets:** Studies use datasets like **RACE**, **SciQ**, **MedMCQA**, **SQuAD**, or domain-specific exam sets (e.g., USMLE, PEBC, PEA) to benchmark automated metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) or conduct human review evaluations (Table 12, Table 13, Table 14, Table 16).

### **4. Evaluation Challenges & Dataset Limitations**

The paper highlights several critical dataset limitations and evaluation bottlenecks:

1. **Transformation Overhead for QA Corpora:** QA datasets (SQuAD, NQs, NewsQA) lack native distractors, forcing systems to build additional candidate selection modules to convert text spans into 4-option MCQs (Section VI, p. 11).
2. **Answer Formatting Complexity:** Answer formats vary widely—from single words and text spans to free-form sentences—introducing significant complexity when attempting to generate grammatically matching, plausible distractors (Section VI, p. 11).
3. **Annotation Biases & Stylistic Variations:** Sourcing data from crowd-workers, Wikipedia, or web queries introduces crowd-worker annotation biases and stylistic quirks that models inadvertently memorize (Section VI, p. 12).
4. **Lack of Standardized Benchmarks:** Section X-C (_"Standardized Evaluation"_, p. 14) explicitly states that a **consistent, universally accepted benchmark dataset for automatically generated MCQs is still missing**. High performance on reading comprehension datasets (like SQuAD or RACE) measures text-overlap or reading ability, not educational validity or teaching value.
5. **Out-of-Domain Generalization Failures:** Models trained on general crowd-sourced datasets fail to generalize to specialized technical disciplines (such as medicine, law, or engineering) without domain-specific data (Section V-F, p. 11; Section X-C, p. 14).
